## Events in order
1. p02 (flash-forward): Rain outside "Shore & Son Meats, 76 Fulton Market". An off-panel voice says "Bang. You're dead." and asks a soaked Dizzy, who is near a Smith & Wesson pistol, "What's it feel like to be a ghost, Dizzy?" Caption: "It's a long story." (p03: "...mine starts here.")
2. p03–p04: In the prison shower, inmates tell Dizzy she's being "sprung" today. Shygirl asks her to give something to "my man Freedy": "This." (seems to be a kiss; [unclear]).
3. p05: In her cell, Dizzy talks to a photo of Hector and Santiago ("I haven't paid for my crimes... and I never will"; "How can God forgive me for your deaths?"). A guard says "Let's go, Cordova. You're free." She answers "I will never be free." Someone replies "You got that wrong."
4. p06–p07: On the El (Clark and Lake), Graves boards with an attaché case, sits beside Dizzy and says "Hector and Santiago. Too bad about what happened."
5. p08: He calls himself Agent Graves and recites her record: arrested at 11 for shoplifting, arrested again 7 hours later for trying to burn down the store she was caught robbing, four years of juvie and gang activity, settled down at 15 (pregnant). Dizzy asks if he's a cop ("Five-oh?").
6. p09: Married Hector at 16. "One night in July" she was a passenger with her homegirls. Her four armed homegirls, an innocent bystander and the two "away" girls who fired on their car were all killed. As the sole survivor she "caught the rap": sentenced to 15 years, released early because of overcrowding. Graves shows her a photo of two men.
7. p09–p10: Graves says these two men killed her family: "This one was driving... and that one was firing", and calls them "Crooked cops" ("Five-O"). Dizzy had believed the Vice Lords did it as "payback for what I'd done."
8. p11: The attaché holds "irrefutable evidence", a gun and 100 rounds, all untraceable. If she acts she has "carte blanche": no law enforcement agency can touch her, and if she uses "terminal force" all investigations will stop once the bullets are retrieved. It is for her eyes only, or there will be "grave repercussions". Asked why, he says "They're the bad guys." He gets off around the Ashland stop.
9. p12–p13: Emilio's crew (Eight and others) works Riza's "shopping list" outside Brio Cafe. Emilio puts on a valet's red vest and drives off in a customer's Mercedes.
10. p14–p15: At home, with the attaché on the bed, Dizzy talks to Hector: his and Santiago's deaths were "payback from God". Her little sister Lucy arrives (Momma is out). Lucy misses her nephew and the dead homegirls. Dizzy has the gun tucked in her waistband: "Not everyone's dead... some of 'em still workin' at it..."
11. p16–p17: At the garage, Dizzy greets Freedy, Smack and her brother Emilio, who arrives in the stolen Mercedes ("Not used, Diz—previously owned"). Emilio says Momma wouldn't let him visit her.
12. p18–p19: Dizzy says she feels she killed Hector and Santiago, because they might not have been "out on that corner" if she hadn't been locked up. The crew say the Lords did it while Hector was pushing Santiago in a stroller, that Hector was "legit", and that "Five-Oh" caught up with the shooters and killed them. Dizzy asks, "You sure that's how it went down?" and leaves. Emilio has Smack call Riza.
13. p20–p21: In the street, Officers Swirski and Morgan stop Dizzy ("We're the good guys"; they heard about her parole). They taunt and grope her, find the gun ("She's packin'!") and say carrying it is a parole violation.
14. p22 (cutaway): Riza pays Emilio "in full" for the Mercedes. Smack puts new Illinois tags on it, and Tweety is to drive it to Kentucky to be broken down. Emilio has a case full of cash; he says it's "my savings" and "My back's covered."
15. p23: With Dizzy cuffed on the car hood, one cop reports that dispatch ran a check and they must let her go "and give her the piece back". The other cop threatens her and vows to find out how she "got a free pass to carry".
16. p24: Back in her room with the gun and attaché, Dizzy tells Hector "They lemme go... It don' make sense... none of this does. Not unless..." She handles the gun and the case ("No.") and cries. "CONTINUED."

## Ending / cliffhanger
Released by the cops with her gun returned, Dizzy sits alone with the attaché and gun. She realizes that none of it makes sense "Not unless..." Graves's offer is real. She says "No." and weeps. Whether she will use the gun is left unresolved ("CONTINUED"). The p02 flash-forward at the Fulton Market meat company ("Bang. You're dead." / "ghost") is not explained in this issue.

## Easy to get wrong
- Two different past events: (a) the July shootout, where Dizzy was a passenger, her homegirls and others died, and she alone survived and went to prison; and (b) the later drive-by that killed Hector and Santiago while she was inside. She was not present at her family's deaths.
- Hector = husband, Santiago = baby son (both dead). Emilio = brother, Lucy = little sister (both alive). ComicInfo lists "Emilio Cordova" and "Lucy Cordova", but neither is part of the dead "family".
- The issue never says outright that Swirski and Morgan are the two men in Graves's photos. The cover and the staging (her eye close-up on p20) suggest it, but the pages don't confirm it. Dizzy does not shoot or kill them in this issue.
- Who was blamed for the killing: Dizzy believed the Vice Lords did it as payback. Graves says crooked cops. The crew says the Lords did it and the cops then killed the shooters.
- The cops do not send her back to prison or keep the gun. A dispatch check forces them to release her and return it.
- Slang: "Five-O"/"Five-oh" = police (on p08 Dizzy is asking whether Graves is a cop). "Sprung" = released. "Packin'"/"piece"/"heat" = carrying a gun. "Chedda" = money. "Sixty Tray" / "All day" = gang call-and-response on the train. "away girls" = rival gang girls. "jackin' rec" [unclear].
- Who said what: Shygirl (an inmate) asks Dizzy to give something to Freedy; Freedy is a man at the garage, not an inmate. Emilio, not Dizzy, deals with Riza. Lucy, not Dizzy, names Baby Chick, Tia and Sad Eyes.
- Graves is only ever "Agent Graves". The issue doesn't say what agency he's from, his first name, or why he's doing this (beyond "They're the bad guys").
- Later-series lore NOT in this issue: the Trust, the Minutemen, Lono, Shepherd, Croatoa, Graves's past, what the "100 bullets" ultimately mean, Dizzy's later role. None of it should appear.
- The prison is "the Women's Correctional Facility in Stateville". The city is never named in the lettering; Chicago is implied by the El stops (Clark and Lake, Ashland) and Fulton Market.
- The cover image (Dizzy firing, the circled cops) shows things that don't happen in this issue.

## Reference synopsis
Isabelle "Dizzy" Cordova, 23, was paroled early from prison, still grieving her husband Hector and baby son Santiago, killed in a drive-by while she was inside. On the El train, a man calling himself Agent Graves recited her criminal past and showed her photos of two men he said killed her family: crooked cops. He gave her an attaché case holding proof, an untraceable gun and one hundred rounds, promising no law enforcement agency could touch her if she used them. Dizzy, who had believed the Vice Lords killed Hector as payback, went home to her little sister Lucy, then visited her brother Emilio's car-theft crew, who insisted the Lords were responsible. Officers Swirski and Morgan then stopped and groped her, found the gun and cuffed her, but a dispatch check forced them to release her and return it. Alone, Dizzy began to accept the offer was real, still undecided.
